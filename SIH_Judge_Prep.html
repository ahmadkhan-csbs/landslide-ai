🏆 SIH 2026 Judge Preparation Guide

NER Landslide AI - Potential Questions & Detailed Explanations

1. Problem Statement & Impact 🌍

Q1: Why did you choose this problem statement, and how is your solution different from existing systems?
Answer: North East India (NER) is highly prone to landslides due to its Himalayan terrain and heavy monsoons, causing massive damage and loss of life. Current systems are largely reactive (responding after the disaster) and data is fragmented.

Our solution is proactive and end-to-end. We differentiate by combining an AI risk engine (Random Forest) for 72-hour early screening, a citizen crowdsourcing portal for ground-truth verification, and an Offline-First PWA (Progressive Web App) that works even during network blackouts in hilly areas.

2. Data Sources & Authenticity 📡

Q2: Landslide prediction heavily relies on accurate data. Where are you getting your data from, and how reliable is it?
Answer: Our data pipeline is built with redundancy and authenticity in mind:
  • Historical Training Data: We trained our ML model on the verified NASA Global Landslide Catalog combined with historical NASA POWER rainfall data.
  • Live Weather Data: We have a fallback architecture. Our primary goal is to use the IMD (Indian Meteorological Department) API for official data. If IMD is down or pending approval, the system gracefully falls back to Open-Meteo to ensure 100% uptime.
  • Terrain Data: We use elevation, slope, and terrain fragility indexes.
  • Disclaimer: We are transparent that this is an experimental screening tool, and official warnings must come from SDMA/DDMA (State/District Disaster Management Authorities).

3. Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning 🤖

Q3: Which ML model did you use and why? What are the key features driving the predictions?
Answer: We used a Random Forest algorithm (scikit-learn).
  • Why? Random Forest is excellent for tabular data, handles non-linear relationships well, and most importantly, it provides Explainable AI (XAI). In disaster management, authorities need to know why an alert is generated.
  • Key Features: Our model explains the risk breakdown: Rainfall (57.8%) is the biggest trigger, followed by Terrain fragility (22.4%) and Location (19.8%).
  • Tip for judges: Mention that your "Model v3 candidate" is currently blocked from deployment because you prioritize public safety and require rigorous spatial validation and domain expert review before pushing new models.

4. Practicality & False Positives 🚨

Q4: How do you handle false positives? If the AI predicts a landslide incorrectly, it could cause mass panic.
Answer: This is exactly why we built a Human-in-the-Loop workflow.
  1. Our AI provides a "Screening Level" (High/Medium/Low), not an official warning.
  2. We have a Citizen Reporting Portal where locals upload geo-tagged photos/videos of cracks or minor slopes failing.
  3. These reports go to a Local Reviewer Dashboard (Admin Panel). They default to UNVERIFIED.
  4. Only after an admin reviews the evidence, verifies it with local authorities, and manually queues it, is the dispatch sent via our SMTP/Fast2SMS integration. We never auto-dispatch mass alerts based solely on AI predictions.

5. Technical Architecture (Offline Capabilities) 📶

Q5: In hilly areas like the North East, internet connectivity drops during heavy rains. How will your app work?
Answer: We built the frontend as a Progressive Web App (PWA).
  • We use Service Workers with a Network-First caching strategy.
  • If the network drops, the app still loads from the cache, allowing citizens to view emergency contacts (112, NDRF, SDRF) and safety guidelines.
  • Future Scope: We plan to implement an offline queue for citizen reports, which will automatically sync and upload as soon as the user enters a network zone.

6. Route & Connectivity Impact 🛣️

Q6: How does this help rescue teams (NDRF/SDRF)?
Answer: We built a Road Connectivity Impact Module. When a landslide happens, it's not just a point on a map; it blocks roads.

Our system uses GIS data to map incidents to road corridors. It shows rescue teams if a road is OPEN, CAUTION, or BLOCKED. It calculates an "Emergency Priority Score" based on nearby villages, hospitals, and people at risk, and can suggest alternate safe routes that avoid confirmed blocked segments.

7. Future Scope & Scalability 🚀

Q7: How do you plan to scale this for production?
Answer: While our prototype runs locally with SQLite and FastAPI:
  1. Database: We will migrate to PostgreSQL with PostGIS for advanced spatial queries and zoning.
  2. Storage: Citizen media (photos/mp4) will be pushed to AWS S3 rather than local storage.
  3. Advanced ML: We plan to train a U-Net deep learning model to automatically detect road cracks and anomalies directly from the crowdsourced video uploads.
  4. Hardware: Integrating live IoT Soil-Moisture sensors to add ground-saturation data into our prediction model.
